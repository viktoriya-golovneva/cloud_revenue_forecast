# Methodology
## NimbusCompute B2B Cloud Revenue and Cash Collections Forecast

## 1. Objective

This analysis produces a three-month forecast for:

- June 2026
- July 2026
- August 2026

It provides two related but distinct financial views:

1. **Consumption revenue forecast**  
   The expected value of cloud resources consumed in each service month.

2. **Cash collections forecast**  
   The expected timing and amount of cash received from customers.

The analysis also provides three scenarios:

- **Downside**
- **Base**
- **Upside**

The methodology is designed to be transparent and explainable to Finance and Sales leadership rather than relying on a black-box time-series model.

---

## 2. High-Level Forecast Architecture

The forecast is split into two independent calculation chains.

### 2.1 Consumption revenue

```text
Consumption revenue forecast
=
Organic consumption from existing customers
+
Incremental CRM pipeline contribution
```

### 2.2 Cash collections

```text
Cash collections forecast
=
Collections from accounts receivable existing at the forecast cutoff
+
Collections generated by future arrears consumption
+
Expected contract prepayments
```

This separation is essential because:

- consumption may occur in one month;
- an invoice may be issued later;
- payment may be received in another month;
- prepayment cash may be received before the related services are consumed.

---

## 3. Data Sources

The analysis uses the following source tables:

| Table | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `billing_customers` | Billing-side customer attributes, status, segment, and default payment terms |
| `monthly_usage` | Monthly product usage by billing customer |
| `price_list` | Product list prices and effective dates |
| `contracts` | Contract dates, commitments, discounts, billing frequency, and payment terms |

| `crm_accounts` | CRM accounts and CRM-to-billing customer mapping |
| `crm_opportunities` | New logo, expansion, renewal, and other pipeline opportunities |
| `crm_opportunity_products` | Product family, ramp timing, and product-level estimates |
| `business_events` | Pricing, product, billing, capacity, and sales-policy events |

---

## 4. Forecast Cutoff

The forecast is produced as of:

```text
2026-06-06
```

This date is used because it is the latest invoice date available in the dataset.

Any payment outcome after the cutoff is treated as unknown at forecast time.

For example, an invoice paid in July 2026 is considered open as of June 6, even if its final status in the source extract is `paid`.

---

## 5. Invoice Cleaning and AR Reconstruction

### 5.1 Clean invoice layer

A single cleaned invoice layer is created before any invoice-based calculation.

The cleaning rules are:

1. Deduplicate by `invoice_id`.
2. Exclude `test_invoice`.
3. Exclude `void_pending`.
4. Preserve valid credits and corrections.
5. Use the cleaned layer in payment behaviour, AR, aging, and collections calculations.

Conceptually:

```sql
ROW_NUMBER() OVER (
    PARTITION BY invoice_id
    ORDER BY invoice_date DESC
)
```

Only the first row is retained.

### 5.2 Accounts receivable as of the cutoff

Open AR is reconstructed from dates rather than the final `payment_status`.

An invoice belongs to AR as of June 6 if:

```text
invoice_date <= 2026-06-06
AND
(
    paid_date is null
    OR paid_date > 2026-06-06
)
```

This prevents future information leakage.

---

## 6. Historical Consumption Revenue

Historical consumption revenue is reconstructed from usage, effective prices, and contract discounts.

### 6.1 Usage normalization

Different products store usage in different fields.

| Product | Quantity field |
|---|---|
| `compute_standard` | `compute_hours` |
| `gpu_a100` | `gpu_hours` |
| `gpu_accelerated` | `gpu_hours` |
| `object_storage` | `storage_tb_avg` |
| `network_egress` | `egress_tb` |

These fields are normalized into one `quantity` column.

### 6.2 Effective price

For each product and service month, the applicable price is the latest price satisfying:

```text
effective_from <= service month
```

Price intervals are constructed using the next effective date.

### 6.3 Applicable contract discount

A contract is applicable when:

```text
contract_start <= service month
AND
contract_end >= service month
```

If no applicable contract is found:

```text
discount = 0%
```

### 6.4 Revenue formula

```text
Consumption revenue
=
Usage quantity
× Effective list price
× (1 − Contract discount)
```

---

## 7. Revenue Reconciliation

Calculated usage-based revenue is compared with actual usage invoices.

The reconciliation includes:

- calculated consumption revenue;
- invoiced usage amount;
- absolute difference;
- percentage difference.

This check is used to identify limitations such as:

- customer-specific negotiated pricing;
- minimum commitments;
- billing adjustments;
- credits;
- incomplete contract history;
- grandfathered prices;
- custom billing logic.

The calculated and invoiced values are not expected to match perfectly because the available data does not fully describe every commercial term.

---

## 8. Existing-Customer Organic Forecast

### 8.1 Baseline

The organic forecast is anchored to May 2026, the latest complete historical service month.

The May baseline is constructed at customer level.

Customers observed in the recent active-customer revenue history are crossed with the May anchor month, which allows customers with no May usage record to be represented explicitly as zero rather than silently dropped.

The main baseline includes customers whose billing status is `active`.

Active customers with no revenue record in the recent historical window are not included in the customer spine. This does not change the forecast total because their observed baseline revenue is zero.

### 8.2 Scenario growth rates

The following month-over-month assumptions are applied:

| Scenario | Organic monthly growth |
|---|---:|
| Downside | −2% |
| Base | +2% |
| Upside | +3% |

For forecast month \(t\):

```text
Organic revenue_t
=
May baseline
× (1 + growth rate)^t
```

where:

- June is month 1;
- July is month 2;
- August is month 3.

These are scenario assumptions based on recent observed growth and identified business risks.

---

## 9. CRM Pipeline Contribution

### 9.1 Included opportunities

The model includes all non-renewal opportunities whose expected start month falls within the forecast horizon, except for the explicitly excluded Vertex component opportunities.

Their contribution depends on:

- opportunity stage;
- probability;
- expected start month;
- expected monthly consumption;
- ramp duration;
- scenario.

This means the implemented filter is broader than only New Logo and Expansion labels.

### 9.2 Renewal treatment

Renewal opportunities are not added as full incremental revenue.

Reason:

```text
Existing customer consumption is already represented in the organic baseline.
```

Adding the full renewal estimate would double-count the customer.

A renewal may be used as evidence of retention, but not as a full incremental addition unless it exceeds the existing baseline.

### 9.3 Opportunity persistence

An opportunity contributes from its expected start month onward:

```text
forecast month >= expected start month
```

A July-start opportunity therefore contributes in both July and August.

### 9.4 Ramp

Ramp is modelled linearly using `ramp_month`.

```text
Ramp factor
=
min(
    (months since start + 1) / ramp_month,
    1
)
```

Example:

| `ramp_month` | Start month | Month +1 | Month +2 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 100% | 100% | 100% |
| 2 | 50% | 100% | 100% |
| 3 | 33% | 67% | 100% |

The exact ramp curve is not available, so a linear ramp is used as a transparent approximation.

### 9.5 Scenario treatment

#### Downside

- only Closed Won opportunities contribute;
- all other pipeline stages contribute zero.

#### Base

- pipeline contribution is probability-weighted.

```text
Expected contribution
=
Expected monthly consumption
× Probability
× Ramp factor
```

#### Upside

- Closed Won and Commit opportunities contribute at 100%;
- all other opportunities remain probability-weighted.

---

## 10. Vertex Labs Treatment

Vertex Labs is represented by three CRM opportunities:

- `OPP-1035`
- `OPP-1036`
- `OPP-1037`

The business-event data indicates that sales packages may have been split into component opportunities.

To avoid double counting:

- `OPP-1037` is treated as the umbrella opportunity;
- `OPP-1035` and `OPP-1036` are excluded.

This is an explicit assumption and requires Sales validation.

### 10.1 Vertex consumption

The monthly consumption estimate is included from the expected start month onward, with probability and ramp adjustments.

### 10.2 Vertex prepayment

For annual prepayment, the expected first-year cash amount is calculated as:

```text
Expected annual prepayment
=
Expected committed spend
× 12
/ Contract term in months
```

For Vertex:

```text
13.8M × 12 / 24 = 6.9M
```

Scenario treatment:

| Scenario | Vertex cash |
|---|---:|
| Downside | 0 |
| Base | 6.9M × 90% = 6.21M |
| Upside | 6.9M |

Vertex is shown separately as a conditional component because it represents a material share of the Base cash forecast.

---

## 11. Cash Collections Forecast

The cash forecast contains three non-overlapping components.

---

## 12. Component A — Existing AR

### 12.1 Normal AR

For non-materially-overdue invoices, expected collection date is approximated as:

```text
due date + 7 days
```

This assumption reflects the historical median payment delay.

### 12.2 Overdue AR

Invoices for which the normal expected payment date is already before the forecast horizon are treated as overdue.

Normal AR and overdue recovery are mutually exclusive.

### 12.3 Aging buckets

Overdue invoices are grouped into:

| Aging bucket | Base recovery assumption |
|---|---:|
| 1–30 days | 80% |
| 31–90 days | 50% |
| 91–180 days | 20% |
| 180+ days | 5% |

These rates are scenario assumptions, not empirically estimated recovery probabilities.

Downside uses half of the Base recovery rates.

Recovered overdue amounts are distributed across the forecast period.

---

## 13. Component B — Collections from Future Usage

### 13.1 Historical payment lag

Payment lag is estimated using mature historical cohorts only.

The training sample is restricted to service months sufficiently far before the cutoff to reduce right censoring.

Only payments recorded by the forecast cutoff are used:

```text
paid_date <= 2026-06-06
```

The model calculates:

```text
Collection lag
=
Paid month − Service month
```

The distribution is weighted by invoice amount.

The main forecast uses the observed collection shares after one and two months.

### 13.2 Existing-customer billing structure

Organic consumption is classified by billing structure:

- monthly;
- PAYG;
- quarterly;
- annual prepay;
- commit drawdown.

Treatment:

```text
Monthly / PAYG
→ historical service-to-cash lag

Quarterly
→ historical lag approximation because the exact quarterly cycle phase is unavailable

Annual prepay
→ no incremental monthly cash from consumption

Commit drawdown
→ no incremental cash unless a separate top-up or prepayment event exists
```

### 13.3 Limitation

The current implementation uses the latest available active-status contract to classify some existing-customer billing frequencies.

Because contract statuses may be stale, this can shift a limited amount of cash between July and August.

It does not affect the consumption forecast.

---

## 14. Component C — Pipeline Prepayments

### 14.1 Annual prepayment

```text
Annual prepayment
=
Expected committed spend
× 12
/ Contract term
```

### 14.2 Quarterly prepayment

```text
Quarterly prepayment
=
Expected committed spend
× 3
/ Contract term
```

Fallback:

```text
Expected monthly consumption × 3
```

### 14.3 Timing assumption

Prepayments are assumed to be invoiced and collected in the expected start month.

The dataset does not provide the exact future invoice date or payment date.

This assumption may shift cash between adjacent months.

### 14.4 Scenario treatment

- **Downside:** only prepayments associated with Closed Won opportunities are included.
- **Base:** prepayments are probability-weighted.
- **Upside:** Closed Won and Commit prepayments are included at 100%; other stages remain probability-weighted.

---

## 15. Scenario Definitions

### Downside

- Organic revenue declines by 2% month over month.
- Only Closed Won opportunities are included.
- Vertex is excluded.
- Overdue recovery rates are reduced to half of Base assumptions.
- Only Closed Won prepayments are included.

### Base

- Organic revenue grows by 2% month over month.
- Pipeline is probability-weighted.
- Ramp is applied.
- Vertex is included at 90% probability.
- Base aging-recovery assumptions are used.

### Upside

- Organic revenue grows by 3% month over month.
- Closed Won and Commit opportunities are included at full value.
- Other stages remain probability-weighted.
- Full Vertex prepayment is included.
- Base aging-recovery assumptions are used.

The final exported output contains nine rows:

The monetary fields are expressed in thousands of US dollars.

```text

```

## 17. Validation and Calculation Controls

The analysis includes checks or explicit calculation restrictions for:

- unique invoice IDs after cleaning;
- exclusion of test invoices;
- exclusion of `void_pending` invoices;
- payment-lag observations restricted to payments recorded by the forecast cutoff;
- no overlap between normal AR and overdue recovery;
- expected number of forecast rows;
- no missing values in the final output;
- reconciliation of cash components to total cash;
- exclusion of both Vertex umbrella and child opportunities from simultaneous counting;
- non-overlapping contracts in the current dataset.

## 19. Limitations

### CRM quality

The pipeline forecast depends on:

- sales probability;
- opportunity stage;
- expected start date;
- opportunity duplication;
- payment structure.

### Contract status quality

Some contract status values are not fully aligned with contract dates.

### Billing-cycle phase

The exact quarterly billing-cycle phase is unavailable.

### Customer-specific pricing

The data does not fully capture:

- negotiated unit prices;
- minimum commitments;
- custom billing clauses;
- grandfathered prices;
- contract amendments.

### AR recoverability

Historical recovery outcomes are not available, so recovery rates are assumptions.

### Prepayment timing

The exact future invoice and payment dates are unavailable.

### Churn status

Some churned customers still have recent usage, suggesting status timing may be imperfect.

### GPU migration and capacity constraints

Historical GPU trends contain product migration and supply constraints, limiting direct extrapolation.

---

## 20. Why This Approach Was Chosen

A pure statistical time-series model was not used as the primary approach because the forecast horizon is short and the main drivers are operational:

- customer-level usage;
- contract terms;
- pricing changes;
- CRM pipeline;
- payment structures;
- AR aging;
- large individual deals.

The selected hybrid framework is more explainable and better aligned with Finance and Sales decision-making.

It allows leadership to understand:

- what portion of growth comes from the existing base;
- what portion depends on pipeline;
- what portion of cash is already invoiced;
- what portion depends on prepayments;
- which assumptions create the largest risks.

## 22. Summary

The methodology creates an explainable B2B cloud forecast by connecting:

```text
Usage
→ Consumption revenue
→ Billing structure
→ Invoice timing
→ Cash collection
```

Each major output can be decomposed into:

- existing-customer organic activity;
- CRM pipeline;
- existing AR;
- future arrears collections;
- prepayments;
- overdue recovery.

The model is designed as a reproducible decision framework that can be updated when new usage, invoice, contract, and CRM information becomes available.
